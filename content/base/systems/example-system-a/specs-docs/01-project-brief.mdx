## Problem

Declarations are processed in overnight batches. A declarant who makes a formal mistake learns
about it the next morning, by which time the consignment is already at the border. The cost lands
on hauliers as waiting time and on the border posts as congestion.

## Who it is for

| User | What they need |
| --- | --- |
| Declarant / freight forwarder | A decision within seconds, and a rejection they can act on immediately |
| Customs officer | Risk decisions that arrive with their reasons attached |
| Risk analyst | Rule changes that take effect without waiting for a software release |

## Scope

**In:** electronic declaration intake, validation against customs rules, risk assessment, the
clearance or hold decision.

**Out:** duty and tax calculation, physical control execution, traveller-facing information. Each
is owned elsewhere and stays there — the reasoning is in
[Scope and Boundaries](/systems/example-system-a/system-architecture/context-and-scope/scope-and-boundaries/).

## Success criteria

| # | Criterion | Measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S-1 | Declarants get a decision while the consignment is still in transit | 95 % of declarations decided within 8 seconds |
| S-2 | Rejections are actionable without a phone call | Support contacts about unclear rejections below 5 per week |
| S-3 | Rule changes do not need engineering | Median time from analyst decision to effect below 1 hour |
| S-4 | Every decision survives legal review | 100 % of sampled decisions reproducible in the annual rehearsal |

## Constraints taken as given

Swiss data residency, the ten-year retention obligation, the externally fixed national message
specification, and the central platform's approved technology set. They are architecture
constraints rather than product choices, and are documented as such in
[2. Architecture Constraints](/systems/example-system-a/system-architecture/architecture-constraints/).
