## The cut follows data ownership

Example System A is decomposed along the lifecycle of a declaration: one component owns the
declaration and its state, another owns the risk decision. The split follows **the ownership of
the data, not the technical layers**, which is why neither component reads the other's database
and why there is no shared "domain" library between them.

The alternative — an intake layer, a rules layer and a persistence layer as separate deployables —
was rejected early. Layer-shaped components force every business change through every deployable,
which is the opposite of what the changeability goal asks for.

## Why the decomposition stops here

A third component for notification was considered and rejected: notifications are a thin
projection of declaration events, and a separate deployable would have added an operational unit
without adding an ownership boundary. The projection lives inside
[Example Component A](/systems/example-system-a/components/example-component-a/).

This is a reversible decision, and it is recorded as such: debt item **D-1** in
[11. Risks and Technical Debt](/systems/example-system-a/risks-and-technical-debt/) says what
would make the team revisit it — a third notification channel.

## The rule for adding a component

A new deployable has to bring a new **ownership boundary**: data that some team is accountable
for and that nobody else may write. "It would scale better" and "it is a different technology"
have both been proposed and both been refused, because neither creates a boundary — they create
an operational unit somebody has to run at three in the morning.
